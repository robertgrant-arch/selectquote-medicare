Medicare Basics

What Is Medicare?

Medicare is the federal health insurance program signed into law in 1965. It primarily serves Americans age 65 and older, as well as those with End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD) — regardless of age.

  ESRD Eligibility: Individuals diagnosed with End-Stage Renal Disease qualify for Medicare regardless of age. Certain disabilities also qualify after 24 months of Social Security Disability benefits.

Enrollment

When & How to Enroll

Your enrollment path depends on your situation. Here are the three most common scenarios.

1

Already Receiving Social Security

If you are already receiving Social Security benefits when you turn 65, you will be automatically enrolled in Medicare Parts A and B. Your Medicare card will arrive in the mail approximately 3 months before your 65th birthday.

  • Automatic enrollment in Part A & B
  • Card arrives ~3 months before birthday
  • You may opt out of Part B if still working
2

Not Receiving Social Security Yet

If you have not yet filed for Social Security, you must manually enroll during your Initial Enrollment Period (IEP) — the 7-month window around your 65th birthday (3 months before, month of, 3 months after).

  • Online at ssa.gov
  • Phone: 1-800-772-1213
  • Mail your application
  • In person at your local SSA office
3

Still Working on Group Coverage

If you have creditable coverage through an active employer (you or spouse), you may delay Part B without penalty. The Rule of 20 applies: if your employer has 20+ employees, your group plan is primary.

  • You have a Special Enrollment Period (SEP) after leaving employer coverage
  • Rule of 20: <20 employees = Medicare is primary

⚠️ HSA Warning: If contributing to a Health Savings Account (HSA), you must stop contributions 6 months before enrolling in Part A (which can be backdated 6 months). Continuing contributions after enrollment triggers a 6% IRS penalty on excess contributions.

Critical Gaps

What Medicare Doesn't Cover

Most people are shocked to discover how much Medicare leaves uncovered. These gaps can lead to financial devastation without proper supplemental coverage.

Original Medicare Has NO Out-of-Pocket Maximum

Part B coinsurance is 20% with zero cap. A $500,000 medical bill = $100,000+ out of your pocket. This is the single biggest financial threat to Medicare beneficiaries.

Long-Term Care

  • Zero days of custodial care covered
  • Skilled nursing limited — see costs below
  • After day 100: you pay 100% of all costs

  • Experimental treatments not covered

Heart Attack / Stroke

  • No cash benefit for ongoing bills during recovery
  • Lost income during recovery not covered
  • Rehabilitation costs can escalate quickly
  • Home modifications not covered

Dental, Vision & Hearing

  • Routine dental care: $0 covered
  • Eyeglasses and routine eye exams: $0
  • Hearing aids (avg. $4,700 per pair): $0
  • These are common senior healthcare needs

Hospital & ER Copays (MA Plans)

  • MA plans have copays per hospital admission
  • ER copays typically $90–$120 per visit
  • Multiple hospitalizations = compounding costs
  • These don't stop at any cap with some plans

Custodial vs. Skilled Nursing — What's the Difference?

Skilled Nursing — Medicare Covers

  • Physical therapy after surgery
  • IV medications and injections
  • Wound care by licensed staff
  • Speech therapy after stroke
  • Requires a doctor's order

Custodial Care — Medicare Does NOT Cover

  • Help with bathing and dressing
  • Assistance eating and toileting
  • Supervision for dementia/Alzheimer's
  • Mobility assistance / transferring
  • This is 70% of all nursing home care

Coverage Options

Your Two Main Paths

There are two primary ways to supplement your Original Medicare coverage. Each has distinct advantages depending on your health needs, budget, and lifestyle.

Option 1

Medicare Supplement (Medigap)

Pairs with Original Medicare + standalone Part D. Covers your cost-sharing gaps.

Advantages

  • Any doctor who accepts Medicare — no network
  • No referrals needed for specialists
  • Predictable monthly costs
  • Plan G: ~$125–200/mo, covers almost everything after $283 Part B deductible
  • Plan N: ~$20–30 less/mo than Plan G, with $20 doctor / $50 ER copays
  • Strong out-of-pocket protection

Limitations

  • Higher monthly premium vs. Advantage
  • Dental, Vision, Hearing NOT included
  • Requires separate Part D drug plan
  • Plan N has Part B excess charge exposure
Option 2

Medicare Advantage (Part C)

Private plan that replaces Original Medicare. Often includes extra benefits.

Advantages

  • Usually $0 monthly premium
  • Maximum out-of-pocket protection built in
  • Often includes dental, vision, and hearing
  • OTC benefits, gym memberships
  • Part D drug coverage usually included
  • Great for those with low healthcare utilization

Limitations

  • Must use network doctors (HMO/PPO)
  • May require referrals for specialists
  • Prior authorization for some services
  • Copay-based — costs add up with heavy use
